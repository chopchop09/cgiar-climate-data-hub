For the decision meeting

Ten decisions, and what each one changes

Every pin on this concept points at one of these. Each carries the options as they stand and the note in the feedback record it comes from, so the meeting argues about the choice and not about what was said. D1 and D8 are the two that cannot be deferred.

D1. Who is the primary user, this year

Decided by: Michelle to decide, in the meeting. Ibukun to challenge.
  • A CGIAR programme scientist inside the portfolio: Breeding for Tomorrow, the GCF Support Facility, the Sustainable Farming Programme workstreams. Named in Mural round 2.
  • Senior leadership and the investment audience: recorded separately in round 2 with a distinct need, getting information out of TAAT, ACCRA and WOCAT and knowing whether those resources connect to climate.
  • A national partner or planning ministry in a Tier 1 country.
  • An external researcher or postgraduate looking for CGIAR climate data.
The design strategy note of 09/08/2026, section 3.2, records who was named in Mural round 2 but does not rank them. Ibukun, 01/09/2026: the site must be opinionated and built for a specific person, and too many features confuse even the ideal user. Every other decision on this page is easier once this one is taken, which is why it is first. The question is who the primary user is this year, not in 2030.

D2. What the spine is

Decided by: Michelle, on the evidence of D1.
  • The five areas of work published on cgiar.org. Externally verifiable. The home page of this concept uses them.
  • The ten editorial themes built from item tags, mirroring gender.cgiar.org. On the More page.
  • Both, with one demoted to a filter.
Climate Hub handover of 12/08/2026, section 4, lists this as open. Peter Steward, 11/08/2026: “The tab themes overlap a lot, need more diffierentiation.” No CGIAR climate-terms taxonomy exists, which is recorded as outstanding in the design strategy note.

D3. What earns a place in ‘Start with these four’

Decided by: Michelle to rule; the rule then applied by whoever owns content under D6.
  • A stated, checkable rule, for example: used by more than one area of work, updated at least annually, and open licence.
  • An editorial pick refreshed each quarter by a named person.
  • Drop the block entirely and let search do the work.
Brayden Youngberg, 13/08/2026: “Possibly this should just highlight some key foundational datasets/tools” and “We just need to define what goes here vs the technical catalog.” The four currently shown (CHIRPS, ERA5, AgERA5, GLW4) were chosen by the build team with no stated rule. This is the only block on the home page where the site takes a view rather than listing, so it is the block that makes the site opinionated or does not.

D4. The name

Decided by: Michelle, with whoever owns programme communications.
  • CGIAR Climate Hub, the current name.
  • Something more specific to what it holds.
  • Defer, and launch under the current name.
Climate Hub handover of 12/08/2026, section 4: “The naming ruling. Still unresolved from the design strategy note.” Mural round 2 listed ‘a wildly generic name’ under what the site should not have. Renaming after launch costs more than renaming before it, so this is cheap now and expensive later.

D5. Where this site ends and the Climate Data Hub begins

Decided by: Michelle with the SO team, before handover.
  • The Hub is the front door, the Data Hub is the technical layer beneath it, and the Hub links out.
  • The Hub sits inside Area of Work 1 and serves all five areas; the Data Hub is infrastructure serving the same five and is not a sibling brand. This is the reading in the design strategy note.
  • Merge them.
Six notes across four sections of the 11/08 and 13/08/2026 reviews asked where one ends and the other begins. The design strategy note of 09/08/2026, section 2, takes position two and records that the original framing was invented locally and is not reflected in published programme documentation.

D6. Who writes the copy, and who refreshes it

Decided by: Michelle to name a person and a cadence.
  • A named editor inside the programme with a monthly cadence.
  • The SO team, once the CMS is in place.
  • Rotate across the five areas of work.
  • Nobody, and the site carries only content generated from the catalogue.
Ibukun, 01/09/2026: the AI-generated text is poor quality and needs replacing. Brayden Youngberg, 13/08/2026: “How do we keep this up to date and managed? ... if it falls out of date it will look abandoned and people will stop looking.” Concept E answers the first half by removing every unsourced sentence and marking the gap in yellow rather than filling it with plausible prose. It cannot answer the second half, because that needs a name.

D7. Whether V1 lists people at all

Decided by: Michelle.
  • Yes, with search, and with each person's consent recorded.
  • Yes, as a plain list, deprioritised as it is here.
  • No, and route enquiries to one contact address instead.
Round 3 of the Mural review, 29/07/2026, criticised the experts page of the model site for having no way to search it. Concept C reproduced that fault deliberately. It is still unfixed here, on the More page, because fixing it is only worth doing if the answer to this decision is yes.

D8. Whether V1 ships before ingestion and tagging are fixed

Decided by: Michelle, and this is the harder of the two decisions in the meeting.
  • Ship V1 now and fix the catalogue after launch.
  • Fix ingestion and tagging first, then ship.
  • Ship with a stated scope: this is what is catalogued so far, and here is what is coming.
Climate Hub handover of 12/08/2026, section 5: searching drought returns three resources and no project, although drought was the most recurrent topic in the check-in of 10/08/2026; metadata completeness across six fields averages 4.6 of 6; only 5 of 14 datasets are complete; Nigeria is the only African country named in the item fields across the whole catalogue. The handover warns that choosing a concept is the easier decision and will crowd this one out if allowed to. Put it on the agenda before the design, not after.

D9. What leaves the site, as opposed to leaving the home page

Decided by: Michelle, with Ibukun.
  • Nothing. Everything on the More page stays, deprioritised.
  • Retire the spotlight and the experts list until they have an owner.
  • Retire whichever spine loses decision D2.
The agreement of 01/09/2026 was that features serving secondary users stay on the site but come off the landing page. That is what Concept E does. It is worth asking separately whether some of them should go, because a page nobody owns is a page that dates.

D10. When the SO team takes the prototype, and what a CMS has to allow

Decided by: John to agree with the SO team once V1 is locked.
  • Hand over at V1 lock, with the CMS decision deferred to the CG website system.
  • Hand over only once a CMS is in place, so no content edit needs a developer.
  • Keep the static build for V1 and migrate at V2.
From the meeting notes of 01/09/2026: the SO team handles integration once a locked prototype is handed over, a CMS is needed long term so that non-designers can manage content, and it will likely sit within the CG website system. John stays involved after the pod for design updates and user engagement tracking, not coding.
What this page is not. It is not a recommendation. Concept E takes a position on every one of these only so that the meeting has something specific to reject. Where a decision changes what appears on screen, the pin shows you exactly where.
