Get involved

Two notes on the review board said this route was missing and that “contact us/get involved needs unpacking”. Unpacking it means saying what a use case is, what is wanted, and what is offered in return.

What a use case is

A named piece of CGIAR work that needs climate data or climate expertise to move forward, with someone accountable for it. It is not a dataset and not a paper. Each one on this site records its area of work, its state, its champion and the catalogue items it draws on.

What we are looking for

  • A decision or output that is currently blocked on climate evidence
  • A named person who will carry it
  • The programme, initiative or centre it sits under
  • A rough sense of the geography and the timeline

What we can offer

  • Help identifying and accessing the right climate datasets
  • Method review, including where an approach is not defensible
  • Connection to others working on the same question
  • A place on this site once the use case is under way

How to start

Contact the Climate Action programme through the CGIAR Climate Action page. No form is mocked up here: a form that submits nowhere would be the kind of fluff this review round asked to remove.

People

Use-case champions

This is not an expert directory. The January 2026 architecture had an expert finder, a support desk and a forum as first-class parts of the Hub, and the review of 11/08/2026 asked for them again: “No community or forum, how do people connect? perhaps this would be part of the missing expert finder.” No directory content has been compiled, so none is shown. What exists is the list of people named against the current use cases.

Asked for, not built

What the board asked for that this concept does not do

Listed rather than mocked up, because each would need content or infrastructure that does not exist, and a plausible-looking mock-up of it would mislead the review.

An expert finder and a forum

No directory has been compiled beyond the six champions above, and a forum needs moderation and an owner. Peter Steward also asked whether an expert finder is even the remit of a single pod in a single programme area, which is a scope question before it is a design one.

Ask the Hub, the chatbot

Drew the heaviest criticism on the board, from both reviewers: cost, sustainability, the development burden, key management, cost protection, and whether it beats a well-crafted prompt in a general AI tool. It is not in this concept.

The flyer builder

Judged safer than the chatbot because it has no free-text input and its results can be cached, but still an infrastructure and cost commitment. The alternative proposed on the board, publishing it as a prompt or skill people run in their own AI tool, is cheaper and is not a page on this site.

Country profiles

The programme publishes a count of 20 priority countries but not, in what was read on 10/08/2026, the names, and no country-level figures have been compiled. No country selector appears anywhere on this site for that reason.
