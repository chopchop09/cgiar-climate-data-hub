Frequently asked questions

Questions the Hub is repeatedly asked, with the honest answer rather than the flattering one.

Draft copy, for the group to edit. The questions below are the ones that recur in review meetings. The answers are a first pass written on 10/09/2026 and are not cleared by CGIAR communications. Where an answer would need a figure, a commitment or an owner, it says so rather than inventing one.

CGSpace is CGIAR's institutional repository: it holds the full record, and it is designed to be complete rather than selective. The Hub is the opposite by design. It is a curated front door that points at a small number of high-value climate assets and explains what each is for, then links out to CGSpace and to the providers themselves.

The Hub does not hold copies of the material, and it is not trying to be exhaustive. If you want everything, go to CGSpace. If you want to know which three things to read first, start here.

This is the open question, and it is the one the design process has been stuck on. The candidates discussed so far are a CGIAR researcher looking for an asset outside their own centre, a national partner or ministry analyst, and a donor or investment officer building a case.

The working position is that a site built for everyone serves nobody, so V1 should be opinionated about one of these and let the others be served second. That decision is not yet made, and until it is, this answer stays as an open question rather than a claim.

The intended route is a short submission form asking for the minimum metadata the catalogue records: what it is, who publishes it, spatial resolution, time coverage, update cadence, licence, formats, and a working link. Fields nobody has recorded are left empty and shown on screen as "not recorded" rather than filled in plausibly.

Who reviews submissions, and how quickly, is not yet decided. Until it is, this answer should not promise a turnaround time.

Behind the desk

Who would actually answer

Four roles the Front Desk implies. None of these is filled, and the feature should not ship until they are, because the concierge idea only works if someone is standing there.

C
Concierge
Reads incoming questions and routes them. Unfilled.
E
Editor
Owns the FAQ wording and keeps it true. Unfilled.
R
Catalogue reviewer
Accepts or rejects submissions against the metadata rules. Unfilled.
S
Site owner
Decides what changes, and signs it off. Unfilled.
The risk worth naming. A Front Desk raises the expectation that a question gets an answer. If no one is staffing it, the feature converts goodwill into disappointment faster than a plain contact page would. Either the roles above are filled before launch, or the desk ships as routing only, with no promise of a human reply.
