About and contact

What this is, who curates it, and how to reach them

About Us and Contact was the fifth heading proposed in the Lexicon session, and it absorbs the material that has no other home: what the Hub is for, the curation rule, how to contribute, and the sign-in link to the internal platform.

The premise

A curated front door, not another repository

What the Hub does

Points at a small number of high-value CGIAR climate assets and says what each is for, who publishes it, what it covers and how to get at it. Every entry records where its metadata was read from and when, so a reader can check rather than trust.

Where a field has never been recorded it is left empty and shown as "not recorded", rather than filled in with something plausible.

What the Hub deliberately does not do

It does not hold copies of anything, and it does not try to be exhaustive. CGSpace is the institutional repository and remains the complete record; this site links to it rather than reproducing it.

It also does not attempt a climate terms taxonomy. The ten themes on this concept are an editorial grouping built from item tags, and every page that shows them says so.

Curation

How something gets onto the Hub

The rule proposed in the Lexicon session is flagship assets only. What follows is that rule written out far enough to be argued with.

Test one

Would you send a partner to it?

If the honest answer is no, it belongs in the repository rather than on the front door. Being CGIAR work is not by itself a reason to feature something.

Test two

Is it maintained?

A tool with no owner and no update in years is a liability on a curated page. Cadence is a recorded field for exactly this reason.

Test three

Can its metadata be checked?

Resolution, coverage, licence and formats have to be readable from the provider. If they cannot be read, they are recorded as not recorded, and the entry says so on screen.

Open. Who applies these tests, and who can overrule them, is not decided. A curation rule without a named owner becomes whatever the loudest recent request was, which is how the current site ended up needing this exercise in the first place.
Contribute

Getting a dataset, tool or publication listed

The minimum metadata set the catalogue records. A submission missing several of these can still be accepted; the empty fields simply show as not recorded.

What it isTitle, and whether it is a dataset, publication, method, tool or innovation.
Who publishes itThe institution responsible, and an author where there is one.
Spatial resolutionVerbatim from the source. Not applicable is a valid answer.
Time coverageThe period the data or the work covers.
Update cadenceHow often it changes, or that it does not.
LicenceAccess and reuse conditions, as the provider states them.
FormatsHow a user actually gets it: API, web maps, GIS, PDF, and so on.
A working linkChecked at the point of listing, with the date recorded.
There is no submission form yet. Until there is one, and until someone owns the review, this section describes an intention rather than a route. It should not be published in this state.
Contact

Reaching the programme

Ask a question

Questions about the evidence, the catalogue or the site itself go through the Front Desk, which is where the routing and the frequently asked questions live.

Go to the Front Desk

The internal platform

The Climate Action operating system sits underneath this site and is for programme staff. The agreed position is that the public site carries a sign-in link and nothing more, so the platform does not shape the public navigation.

Sign in (mock, not wired up)

One address is proposed, not yet staffed. The wishlist names climate@cgiar.org as the Front Desk mailbox, the place a question goes when the site cannot answer it. Publishing an address commits somebody to answering it, and that person has not been named, so the prototype shows the address only as the hand-off in the Front Desk's "no match" answer and nowhere else.
