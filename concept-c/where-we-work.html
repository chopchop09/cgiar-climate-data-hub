Where we work

Coverage, region by region, including where it stops

Where We Work was one of the five headings proposed in the Lexicon session, and it is the one most likely to be asked for by a national partner. This page shows what the catalogue behind this prototype covers geographically. It is built from the coverage field recorded on each item, so it is a map of the evidence, not a map of the programme.

Response page, the first of a reusable kind

El Niño: the situation and CGIAR's response, by country

A clickable map of Africa, a country summary in eight fixed fields, filters, a print view and the source and date on every claim. Built as a template so the next crisis or campaign gets the same page. One country summary so far, from IITA's own inventory, and it says so.

The published commitment

Twenty priority countries, across Tier 1 and Tier 2

That is what the Climate Action programme publishes on cgiar.org, read on 10/08/2026.

The country list is not on this site, and should not be guessed. The number is published. The named twenty, and which sit in Tier 1 and which in Tier 2, were not read from a CGIAR source when this prototype was built, so nothing here lists them. A live Where We Work page needs that list confirmed by the programme before it is drawn on a map, because a wrong country on a public map is the kind of error partners remember.

The honest reading

What this distribution actually shows

One

Most of it is global

The bulk of the catalogue is global datasets and methods. Useful, but a national partner asking "what do you have for my country" is not served by a global gridded product they then have to clip themselves.

Two

Regional coverage is thin and uneven

Africa and South Asia have atlases. Other regions do not. A page that draws twenty flags and shows an atlas for two of them invites the question of what the other eighteen get.

Three

Country-level pages are a commitment, not a feature

Twenty country pages need someone to keep twenty country pages current. If that owner does not exist, region-level honesty beats country-level decay.

People

Who to talk to in a region

The experts listed on this site are recorded by theme and by centre rather than by country. That is a gap: a partner in a given country usually wants a name before they want a dataset.

Partners

Who CGIAR works with here

Sabrina's wishlist entry asks for some way to feature partners: a partner section inside each regional page, or a separate directory with a map. The country and regional pages the wishlist describes carry work, results, people, partners and resources in one place, linked to the CGIAR Results Dashboard, and the partner block belongs on each of them rather than on a page of its own. Nothing is listed here because no partner record has been read into this prototype; the Nigeria response summary shows what one filled block looks like.
